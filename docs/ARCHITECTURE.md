# Architecture

## Overview

alexnava.me is a static site: two pages, one stylesheet, a small UI script and a deferred Three.js scene, built by esbuild into `dist/` and served by Cloudflare Pages. The page never depends on the scene. The title card (a night sky drawn in CSS, the identity and Contact) is the first visual, behind the opening's paper doors, which part onto it; it is final for static visitors. Capable devices load the scene behind it, reveal it once it can draw, and tour five directed shots, the painted sky (The watch) and a twisted tree with a lantern, joined by three coast shots when the lazy sea and cliffs land. Terms such as film, baseline, startup tier, low tier, commit point and stand-in lantern are defined in the [Glossary](GLOSSARY.md).

The UI and scene bundles meet only on `window.BabelSite`, a few DOM hooks and the scene's `babel:` User Timing entries, which the loading line observes. The UI publishes `BabelSite.ui`, `BabelSite.shared`, `BabelSite.sceneLoader`, `BabelSite.sceneDoors`, `BabelSite.ensureSceneReady` and the quality helpers on `BabelSite.scene`; the doors' own script adds `BabelSite.paperDoors` on the page. The scene entry adds its own entries to `BabelSite.scene`, among them `initHomeScene`, `setVisitorPaused` and `isVisitorPaused`, and publishes `BabelSite.sceneDebug` when asked. [Contracts](CONTRACTS.md) lists every shared name, event, attribute and User Timing entry. `Babel` is the code's own namespace (`BabelSite`, the `babel:` marks and events, the `__BABEL_*__` build-time defines), not the Babel compiler.

## Page and startup

[src/main.js](../src/main.js) boots in this order:

1. `initUi()` wires the hero (`initHeroChrome()`) and then the footer's Contact (`initContact()`, [contact.js](../src/ui/contact.js)): Contact shows, the email address and LinkedIn fold behind it, and an address ending `#about`, `#profile`, `#experience` or `#contact` (or the same with `-text`) opens them ([UI](UI.md#contact)). Neither waits for the scene.
2. After first paint (`requestIdleCallback` with a 500 ms timeout, or `requestAnimationFrame` then `setTimeout` without it) it applies the static gates. Forced colours are checked first: while active, the scene host is hidden without probing WebGL, requesting the scene bundle or models, or starting the loading line. Neither a quality override nor `?sceneDebug=1|true` bypasses this gate. The scene also stays static when reduced motion or reduced data is preferred (including `navigator.connection.saveData`), when WebGL is unavailable or software-rendered (SwiftShader, llvmpipe, Microsoft Basic Render Driver), or when the startup tier is low. `?quality=high|balanced` passes the reduced-motion, reduced-data and software gates. `?sceneDebug=1` passes the same three in main.js, but a reduced-data preference still gives the low startup tier (quality.js `selectSceneQualityTier`), so the title card stays: main.js declines before requesting the scene bundle or, when the probe could not read the texture limits, after downloading it, once `initHomeScene()` declines the low tier. Neither passes the WebGL gate, and `?quality=auto` or an unknown value forces nothing. There is no user-agent, Lighthouse or viewport exception. When forced colours clear, main.js rechecks the remaining gates even with an explicit quality or debug override, starting the scene once if eligible; concurrent recovery events share the startup already in flight. Without an explicit quality or debug override, clearing reduced motion or reduced data also rechecks startup. An explicit `?quality=high|balanced|low` or `?sceneDebug=1|true` determines startup as above (`forcesLiveScene`); clearing motion or data alone does not trigger recovery from a static tier selected under those controls.
3. The startup tier comes from [quality.js](../src/scene/quality.js), which the UI bundle carries, and the limits the WebGL probe already read. Unknown limits leave the choice to the scene, which reads them with a probe of its own and declines the low tier itself (`initHomeScene()` returns `false`).
4. `sceneLoader.begin({ tier })` starts the loading line. main.js appends the scene entry as a module script and, just before it, a `<link rel="modulepreload">` for each chunk the entry imports statically (`__BABEL_SCENE_MODULE_PRELOADS__`: the shared Three.js chunk), so both download together. No lazy chunk is preloaded.
5. The pure [scene-modes.js](../src/scene/scene-modes.js) selector resolves the same opening for UI boot and the renderer. An ordinary live visit opens Lantern study (`tree`, zero-based angle 1) or The watch (`watch`, 0), by chance (the owner's pick of 2026-10-26, "Watch or Lantern by chance"), and The watch on the lean path (an integrated GPU, the owner's call of 2026-10-27) and on a touch screen (a phone or tablet, the owner's call of 2026-10-28), both read through `readStartupQuality()`: main.js `chooseOpening()` rolls once, before the loading line begins and the models are requested, and sets `scene.cinematicSelection` and `cinematicAngle`, which the selector keeps as a script selection. Explicit view and angle links and a selection a script made first retain their meanings and are never rolled; with nothing chosen (the scene loaded without main.js), the selector's default is Lantern study. A selected tree opening requests the startup tier's tree and then lantern at high fetch priority; a Watch opening requests only the tree at low priority (`__BABEL_ARCHITECTURE_PREFETCH_URLS__`). A hidden page waits until shown. Responses are shared on `BabelSite.scene.prefetched`, keyed by URL. [architecture-assets.js](../src/scene/architecture-assets.js) takes a matching response at most once; the rest are aborted when the live selection starts, at teardown, or when the bundle or initialization fails.
6. When the bundle has run, main.js reports `stage("bundle")`, calls `initHomeScene()` and reports `stage("init")`. A failed bundle or initialization hides the host and calls `end("static")`. A failed module load removes its script so a later call can retry.
7. A close tree opening stays behind the title card until its subjects, settled supplied or fallback lantern, coastal terrain, material maps, root and bank detail, foreground coverage through the complete current move, rocks and final shader warm-up are prepared. [opening-preparation.js](../src/scene/opening-preparation.js) owns functional deadlines independently of the loading line: 15 visible seconds without progress or one 30-second visible budget covering capability preparation, entry/shared downloads, synchronous setup and scene preparation. main.js hands the remaining time to the scene through the internal `scene.takeOpeningBudget()`; background time and static-preference pauses are excluded, while preference recovery preserves time already consumed. A pre-entry timeout aborts unused model requests and permanently prevents a late bundle from initializing that attempt; a scene-preparation failure disposes the pending runtime. The tour and its drift remain at phase zero while preparing; reveal resets the native dwell, so Lantern study keeps its complete 6 s hold. A Watch opening, rolled or explicit, retains the smaller subjects-and-shaders gate within the same opening budget.

The capability gate calls `shared.prepareWebGLCapabilities()`. When Worker and OffscreenCanvas are available, a one-shot, fingerprinted same-origin worker runs the shared probe, reads the same renderer and texture limits, and releases its context. A missing, blocked, malformed or timed-out worker (2 s) falls back to the document canvas probe; a worker reporting no WebGL also falls back, since worker support does not establish document support. The result is cached for the scene's synchronous guards. main.js rechecks preference gates after the asynchronous probe before requesting any scene asset, after the bundle downloads, and before initializing an already-loaded entry. A preference that interrupts startup rearms recovery: the next eligible change reuses the downloaded entry and already-started model responses, with no duplicate startup. Deferred hidden-page prefetch is cancelled; ordinary bundle or initialization failures do not install an automatic retry.

### Title card and loading line

Neither page has an `<img>` or `<picture>` element, and the stylesheet requests no picture: its only URLs are the fonts, the opening's paper detail map and `data:` URIs (the night's stars, the grain and the cursor). index.html and styles.css paint the title card: the night gradient, a CSS star field (`.scene-shell::before`) and the vignette under the identity and Contact. A faint grain (`.scene-vignette::before`, a 150px inline SVG tile) dithers the night against banding. It fades with the canvas's reveal through a sibling selector, `.scene-canvas.is-ready + .scene-vignette::before`, so the vignette must directly follow the scene host ([Contracts](CONTRACTS.md#dom)). The 404 page is the same CSS night with one centred line: the eyebrow, a heading in the name's type, a sentence in the intro's and Home in the footer's; it has no scene host, so its grain stays. Its head names the homepage's icons, preloads both fonts, declares `color-scheme: dark` and takes the site's description. It loads no script.

On the live path [src/ui/scene-loader.js](../src/ui/scene-loader.js) (`BabelSite.sceneLoader`) shows one line, `#scene-loader`: a label above the footer and a bar along the window's top edge, both in the name's colour ([UI](UI.md#loading-line)). Its percentage and bar follow real work. The base ledger allocates 10 points to the bundle, 4 to initialization, 70 to required tree bytes and 12 to build; a Watch opening has no required model bytes, so its build takes 82 points. Once preparation is reported, that ledger supplies 80% of the 96-point pre-reveal cap and completed preparation stages supply the remaining 20%. Progress never decreases and no timed animation claims completion. The same validated URL and script selector determines the required roles for the line and the scene. `track(url, response)` counts tree response bytes against the build manifest; the independent preparation snapshots account for lantern completion or fallback and the remaining close detail. Build follows `babel:glb-parse:<role>`, `babel:assembly:<role>` and `babel:shaders:<role>` measures for the watch and selected tree. `preparation(snapshot)` or `babel:opening-preparation` supplies `{ status, completed, total, stage, reason }`; ready work stays below 100% until the canvas is actually revealed, while failure or fallback retires the line. A MutationObserver on `#home-scene` completes it on `is-ready` or retires it on `hidden`; the `babel:reveal` mark backs up the class change. Its decorative watchdog checks every 2 s and retires it after 15 visible seconds without byte, build or preparation progress (16 s at that cadence), emitting `babel:opening-timeout`. The scene's separate preparation clocks and cleanup still operate when the line or its observers are absent. `.scene-canvas` keeps its 480 ms opacity transition.

The line's star, `#scene-star` in the scene shell, stands where the opening shot will show the scene's star (the owner's rule: it has to line up), under the canvas. The scene reports that place through `sceneLoader.star()` in CSS px from the canvas's top-left: index.js `reportLoaderStar()` converts a `lensSource()` result (canvas UV, y up, the radius in frame heights) with the renderer canvas's `getBoundingClientRect()` size. Before the watch commits, `predictLoaderStar()` reports [opening-star.js](../src/scene/opening-star.js) `openingStar()`: from the scene's start (in `applySceneSize()`, once the composition has set the scene's height through `subsystemRegistry.resize`), again on every resize and once the fonts load, until the camera's own report or the reveal. `openingStar()` fits a cinematic camera of its own (cinematic.js `createCinematicCamera`'s `measure` option) to the watch's stored outline ([opening-outline.js](../src/scene/opening-outline.js), opening-star.js `watchMeasure()`, which the scene's camera frames The watch by too) for the asset tier and The watch's resolved variant, with the scene's safe area (`cinematicArea` or `measureCinematicArea`), its ground (`cinematicGroundY`, which the scene's camera reads too), the star's world position and the watch's marker (`watchRoot`, where the lookout's posts stood on the terrace, its world height `watchY`, so the environment root's composition height lifts or lowers it); it applies The watch at time 0 and the tour's opening phase (`cameraTour ? 0 : null`) and projects the star with `lensSource()` before disposing the controller, whose disposal resets the camera's lens. It takes about 0.2 ms, and gives no star when the opening is another shot (anything but the watch's angle 0, from `scene.cinematicSelection` and `cinematicAngle`), the outline is missing or stale for that tier and variant, or the star stands outside the frame. Measured on 30 window shapes (desktops from 1280×720 to 3440×1440, a square, squat windows 560–700 px wide, portrait monitors, phones both ways and tablets), the prediction lands within 0.56 px of the camera's own star. Once the scene's camera has fitted the opening shot itself, the lens block computes the star from the live camera while the canvas is not yet shown and reports that instead (`openingCameraStar`), until the reveal. scene-loader.js warms the star with the real progress, from the progress at which its place first became known to the reveal ([UI](UI.md#loading-lines-star) has the parts and their transforms), writing only transforms and opacities, at most once a frame. At the reveal the line reads 100% and the star is the scene's own as the opening shot shows it, in place and size; the canvas fades in over it, and it hides with the line after 600 ms. A line retired below 100% hides its star at once, and reports after a run has ended are ignored.

A shot that looks at the lantern (`LENS_FX.heat.flameShots`) gives the line the lantern's light instead: its report carries `kind: "lantern"`; on the title card the light is the lantern as the film's lens sees it (the owner's pick of 2026-10-26, "Camera glare": a hot core, an amber bloom, the lens's streak, a candle's flicker) and through the paper its glass's amber ("Lantern glass"), `LANTERN_GLOW` (0.35 world units, about the glass's half-width) about the flame. For a tree opening, `predictLoaderStar()` reports opening-star.js `openingFlame()` from the scene's start, as it reports The watch's star: a cinematic camera of its own fitted, as the scene's camera fits the supplied lantern, to a stand-in built from the lantern's stored outline ([opening-lantern.js](../src/scene/opening-lantern.js), the convex hull of the lantern model as lantern.js `createLanternArchitecture()` normalizes it) standing on the lantern's ground point (estate-layout.js `estateLantern()` at `groundHeight()`, through `environmentRoot`, so the composition's height lifts or lowers it) at prop-scale.js `LANTERN_SCALE`, with the flame, the model's luminous centre, projected by `lensSource()`. The stand-in is slivers from each corner of the hull to its centre, so `measureShot()` measures it as it measures the model. That holds while the shot's form frames the whole lantern (its region the lantern's whole height, no focus): Lantern study in every form, Root and lantern except on landscape phones, where `openingFlame()` gives none and the line waits for the camera's own report. On nine window shapes across both tiers the prediction lands within 0.1 px of a camera fitted to the model, in about 0.5 ms. Once the scene's camera has fitted the shot, the lens block reports the lantern's light (`treeArchitecture.light`) from the live camera until the reveal, as it does the star.

### The opening's doors

`#scene-doors` (index.html, after `.site-shell`) is one sheet of washi torn down the middle, closed over the whole page at the first paint, the identity, Contact and loading line under it (z-index 20, over `main`, the footer and the line, under the skip link; [UI](UI.md#the-openings-doors)); while the line loads, its bar shows on the paper itself (`is-loading`, from the first `light()`). Each half is a whole-window box whose `.scene-doors__paper` is clipped along the tear, so one transform carries it off its side; its `.scene-doors__light` holds the stylesheet's glow and counter-moves about the same corner, so the light stays where the star is while the paper slides past. The tear's polygons in styles.css are cut from the 81 points of [paper-doors.js](../src/doors/paper-doors.js) `TEAR`, and a test holds them to it.

The stylesheet alone opens the halves 4 s after the first paint (their `animation-delay`), so a page whose scripts never run is never held. [src/ui/scene-doors.js](../src/ui/scene-doors.js) (`BabelSite.sceneDoors`) runs before main.js and holds them (`is-held`): the stylesheet's opening moves to an 11 s backstop and pauses while the page is hidden (`is-paused`), and the script's own cap opens them after 10 s of visible time, read on the stylesheet's own clock (the halves' animation, `getAnimations()`), so the cap always comes first. A script that runs after the stylesheet began (3.85 s) leaves it to finish, and so does one whose opening comes after the backstop has begun. They part (`is-open`, keyframes of their own, since no transition starts where the backstop's animation is replaced) when the loading line ends (`end()`: revealed or stalled), on every static path (main.js `disableSceneHost()`), and at once on input while held: a tap on the paper (held doors take the pointer, so its `click` lands on them), a key other than a modifier alone or a shortcut (`keydown`), or keyboard focus on anything but the page itself (`focusin`), so nothing the visitor reaches for waits under the paper. At the reveal they first wait out the canvas's 480 ms fade, so the scene shows as they part. The scene reveals as soon as it draws ([Opening presentation](#opening-presentation)); a tap, key or focus, the cap or the loading line's retirement parts them first, and a scene still preparing then fades in over the title card. Once open they leave the page (`hidden`) after 2.3 s. Reduced motion and forced colours show no doors; increased contrast keeps plain paper.

On the live path `begin()` upgrades them: scene-doors.js loads the doors' own script (`__BABEL_DOORS_URL__`, [doors-entry.js](../src/doors-entry.js)), whose `paperDoors.start()` hands the doors' canvas to a worker running the same script (`transferControlToOffscreen()`), or draws on the page where it cannot. [paper-doors.js](../src/doors/paper-doors.js) draws the halves as kozo washi in WebGL2 over the scan's height and detail maps (`__BABEL_PAPER_URLS__`), its formation, long fibres, bundles and bark flecks in its own px, lit by a lamp above its top and a dim moon, with the scene's star behind them at the loading line's star, or in a lantern opening the lantern's light in its glass's amber (`PAPER.lantern`; `light()` from `paintStar()`, redrawing for it at most every `LIGHT_MS`, 250 ms, since the scene reports the star on each of its hidden opening frames, which must not share the GPU with a redraw of the paper), and draws the opening itself on the stylesheet's timing. A lantern's light flickers as a candle does (`candle()`, within 1 ± 0.22, which also breathes the glow's spread): while it is lit behind the closed doors the sheet redraws `FLICKER_FPS` (15) times a second, which leaves the lantern opening's time to the reveal as it was (24 cost it about 4%). In a worker its frames never wait on the main thread, so the scene's long tasks cannot stall the opening. Once its first frame is up the canvas fades in (`is-drawn`) and the stylesheet's halves leave it alone (`is-drawn-only`); any failure, a lost context included, removes both classes, and the stylesheet's doors stand as they are. `sceneDoors.drawing` reports `drawn`, `loading`, `none` or why the drawing last failed.

`node tools/bake-root-shade.mjs` prints the three constants from both tree variants, deterministically; paste them over the ones in [terrain-build.js](../src/scene/terrain-build.js) whenever one of its inputs changes: the tree models, the tree's seating (`TREE_FOOTING`, `TREE_SINK`), the root lines and lattice, the key-light direction (`KEY_LIGHT`), the lantern's flame (`LANTERN_FOOT`, `LANTERN_IMAGE.glow`), the terrain, or the ground the tables must leave untouched (the lantern clearing about `LANTERN_FOOT`, the river to its reach (`RIVER`, `RIVER_BED`, restating `SLATE_RIVER` and `SLATE_RIVER_BED` in mud-ground.js) and the puddles in `PUDDLE_ZONES`), or the stream's channel (`STREAMS`, restating `SLATE_STREAMS`). `test/terrain-build.test.mjs` re-bakes and fails on any difference.

[opening-outline.js](../src/scene/opening-outline.js) is data, never edited by hand: The watch's stored outline, baked from the lookout's models before the owner left the lookout out (2026-10-20) and kept, so The watch frames as it was composed. For every variant `resolveDirectedShot()` gives The watch over a sweep of window sizes (three today, each keyed by `outlineKey(shot)`: its subject, region, focus, azimuth, height and target height, everything `measureShot()` reads from a shot) it holds each tier's measurement of the lookout (high and balanced), its root at the origin, its points reduced to their convex hull: `fitShot()` tests each point only against linear limits, so the hull fits exactly as all the points do. The tiers fit a few px apart in some variants (short landscape windows), so each tier has its own. Points are stored as little-endian Int16 multiples of `OUTLINE_RESOLUTION` (0.005 world units), base64: about 9.7 kB of source, 4.7 kB gzipped, in the scene's default load. A change to The watch's framing (`DIRECTED_SHOTS.watch[0]` and its variants) that changes a key finds no outline: `test/opening-star.test.mjs` holds every variant of The watch to an entry for both tiers.

[opening-lantern.js](../src/scene/opening-lantern.js) is data too: `node tools/bake-lantern-outline.mjs` prints it from both lantern models (images/architecture/lantern-high.glb and lantern-balanced.glb): the convex hull of each as lantern.js `createLanternArchitecture()` normalizes it (its foot at the origin, `LANTERN_AUTHORING_HEIGHT` tall), its corners as little-endian Int16 multiples of `LANTERN_RESOLUTION` (0.0005 lantern units) sorted by height, base64, and its luminous centre. The tiers share one shape, and the bake fails where they differ. It is about 1.6 kB of source in the scene's default load. `test/opening-star.test.mjs` re-bakes both models and fails on any difference, so a new lantern model needs a new bake.

The film's ground work also prepares before publication. `environment.prepareFilmDetail()` builds detached grass during model downloads through the same seeded generator used by the immediate constructor; the watch waits for it, and film activation only adopts the finished detail. Normal accumulation and bounds run in short slices with the same buffer results. After terrain settlement, optional root litter and rushes build detached in idle slices, free partial geometry on cancellation and join only at existing unseen commit points. Procedural ground canvases start as a small flat preview; full detail is painted only for a failed slate load, a reset out of film, or a visible frame that still needs the procedural maps. Successful slate loads skip that unused painting.

The Python material helpers are offline authoring tools, never build or runtime requirements. `python tools/bake-coast-albedo.py --source PATH --output images/materials/coast-albedo-512.webp` requires the known diffuse source MD5, uses Pillow locally and prints the final WebP's measured linear RGB mean for `COAST_ALBEDO.mean`. [Assets](ASSETS.md) and [Credits](CREDITS.md) describe their source and format contracts.

`python tools/repair-landmark-geometry.py --source-dir PATH --output-dir images/architecture` reads the two original tree GLBs and verifies their SHA-256 hashes. It computes the bounded fork-volume change and affected surface frames, verifies every approved output hash in memory, and then writes the two results; optional `--report` records the checks. Local NumPy is its only authoring dependency. Model lengths, JSON, maps, UVs, indices, AABBs and protected contacts stay fixed; runtime code performs no repair or remeshing.

The close soil shader uses world-anchored photographic grains and shallow relief, with fine cellular crumbs only where resolved. Its near detail fades from 6 to 32 units. On WebGL2 and WebGL1 with explicit texture gradients, expensive close lookups are skipped beyond their fade; gradients are computed before conditional sampling. WebGL1 without the extension retains unconditional filtered sampling. The optional 512px soil roughness owner shares neither success nor failure with the five required slate maps. It keeps a decoded bitmap for context restoration, stages bindings until a cut or still frame, and restores the borrowed uniform before disposal. `createGroundTextures.update` forwards frame eligibility to this owner.

The lazy foreground owner borrows camera fitting, grass material and placement providers through the film terrain library rather than importing entry modules into a shared boot chunk. A temporary camera measures the finite coverage of all authored shots and canonical viewport variants without touching the live camera. A pure `loosePebbleBurial` helper is shared by the legacy fine grit and new field through the terrain provider closure, varying only vertical seating while preserving placement entropy and buffers other than height. World-seeded 8-unit chunks prepare in idle slices; root and water checks test emitted triangle footprints, including their interiors. Publication is cut/still gated; committed chunks then cull continuously with the live camera. Bounded resident geometry, per-tier draw and triangle limits, projected-size fades and short whole-piece fades keep distant detail cheap. A completed optional chunk requests one refinement frame for held scenes. Film withdrawal hides the field and terrain disposal frees its materials and buffers.

## Scene lifecycle

On graphics-context loss, retire owned environment, compositor and shadow targets
while GL is still lost. This removes the old renderer's disposal listeners before
its new context exists. Compositor target and texture identities remain reusable;
the environment capture and shadow map are recreated on restoration, with decoded
assets retained. Ordinary program invalidation does not release live targets.
A tree opening starts its sliced physical ground alongside the initial sky capture;
the Watch assembly never holds that independent preparation. The opening warms
only its sky and compositor initially. Mandatory owner preparation
coalesces intermediate warmups; its final opening compile covers the complete tree
cohort, or only the sky for The watch. Background subjects then warm after reveal,
before their first cut. A scoped compile retains the full scene's lighting while
uploading only its own visible maps. The watch looks wholly above the physical set:
that parent is culled for its sky-only draws and restored at the next cut or on
entering the walk; no physical pixels or camera poses change.

Shader readiness follows the retained Three program wrappers and their cleared
handle fields on destruction. It polls asynchronous completion before link status;
it never calls `gl.isProgram`, whose driver synchronization can stall startup.
Shader waits pause their queries and visible-time budgets while the document is
hidden, so a background tab does not retire still-linking GPU resources.
Task-based compile batches explicitly flush before readiness polling: they have
no animation-frame boundary to submit commands ([WebGL guidance](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebGL_API/WebGL_best_practices#flush_when_expecting_results)).

A replaced ground-shader probe transfers its owned material to the lazy preparation
owner after its bounded admission poll stops. GPU compilation may outlive that
poll: the material stays alive until actual asynchronous completion, context
retirement, or a 15-second visible cleanup bound. Borrowed maps and terrain buffers
are retained by their original owners. This avoids deleting a program while the
background compiler is still using it, without holding the loading UI past its
existing deadline or leaving a raw Three material-property poll active.

- **Subsystems.** Each scene part registers with a registry ([subsystem.js](../src/scene/subsystem.js)) as an object with optional `applyQuality`, `resize`, `update` and `dispose` hooks; a missing hook is a no-op. Hooks run in `lifecycleOrder`. Disposal runs in reverse registration order, continues past a failing subsystem and rethrows the first error. A throw during initialization disposes what was registered, and nothing registers after disposal.
- **Borrowed resources.** A subsystem that derives from another's geometry, materials or programs restores the originals before freeing its own: the film terrain over the procedural ground, the lantern mount over the stand-in lantern, and the slate's shader hooks over the materials they extend. Quality changes and failed or stale loads keep a usable fallback.
- **Shared uniforms.** Some uniform objects are lent rather than copied, so one write reaches every material that reads them; a borrower never frees or replaces them ([Contracts](CONTRACTS.md#shared-uniform-objects)). index.js creates the slate's contacts and text boxes (`groundContacts`, mud-ground.js `createSlateContacts()`) before the sky shell and shares them with the ground, the rocks, the bark (`textGuard`), the sky shell and, through the shell, the stars. The starfield borrows the shell's `uTime`, `uFilm`, `uClouds`, `uNebulaLayers`, `uCloudReshape`, `uVortex`, `slateText` and `slateAspect`; disposing the stars leaves the sky and its uniforms intact. drifting-mist.js lends its `MIST_UNIFORMS` (with the ground's text boxes, `lendMistText()`) to every material the mist reaches.
- **Asset leases.** architecture-assets.js loads the tree, lantern and rocks for the startup tier, and commits the watch (no model) once its stage is ready; hill-silhouette.js loads the three massifs at the same tier through its `loadArchitectureAsset` (fetch priority `low`), outside the leases, and owns their maps until it is disposed; if one fails or misses `MASSIFS.deadline` (12 s), the requests abort and the five procedural rings land instead. A decoded asset is held by a lease through staging, commit and restoration; aborted or stale results are released through the same path. The watch and the tree are required. The tree's parsed asset passes through `architecture.js` `prepareArchitecture()` before these synchronous commit callbacks run. It imports the lazy `architecture-prepare.js` helper after parsing and prepares geometry in bounded tasks; a failed import preserves the synchronous constructor. A private WeakMap holds each prepared geometry and, for the tree, the original and softened normal buffers; the unchanged constructors consume that record and own its disposal. Aborting preparation frees partial geometry. The lantern and rocks retain bounded fallbacks. A close tree opening waits for their prepared or fallback state before revealing; a Watch opening retains their later landing path.
- **Reveal.** Until a directed shot is ready the hidden canvas keeps the pre-reveal orbit camera and redraws only when invalidated. Programs link through `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` (`createShaderWarmup`, each warm-up bounded at 2 s; before the reveal, an opening warm-up still linking at its bound waits again, within the opening's own deadlines, rather than giving up the scene). For the real renderer, `shader-programs.js` queues compilation and snapshots every retained program variant for its materials, then polls those captured programs with a bounded wait that stops on disposal or context loss. It does not follow mutable material properties after a quality change or resource release. `rendering.compileShaders()` awaits scene colour programs, shadow-depth variants prepared on detached geometry probes, and the compositor. The compositor yields before preparing its independent scene-copy, bloom, grading and final-pass program groups for their real render targets, restoring each borrowed target before yielding again, with no added draw; its readiness promise is cached until context loss. Depth materials stay owned by rendering until disposal so the shadow program cache retains their leases. Completed mountains enter the same bounded warm-up before their first visible draw; their entrance fade starts with drawn frames, and a quality change during preparation keeps its current visibility rules. Before the reveal the hidden canvas draws only after the complete opening gate and its final warm-up are ready, so partial terrain cannot enter a blocking program lookup. WebGL1 retains its explicit final preparation draw. The first frame that can show marks `babel:reveal`, adds `is-ready` to `#home-scene` and starts the 480 ms fade-in. Afterwards a committed model stays hidden until its programs are linked.
- **Framing.** Each directed shot is fitted inside a safe area cut from the hero's layout box and the bottom bar's top edge ([cinematic.js](../src/scene/cinematic.js) `cinematicSafeArea`). `isStackedLayout(width, height)` ([directed-shots.js](../src/scene/directed-shots.js), re-exported by cinematic.js for index.js) mirrors the hero's breakpoints in styles.css ([Contracts](CONTRACTS.md#hero-breakpoints)): a stacked layout frames the subject below the name; landscape phones and desktops frame it right of the hero; portrait monitors frame it across the full width above the name. `resolveDirectedShot` gives a shot its viewport variant ([Scene modes](SCENE-MODES.md#viewport-variants)): `portrait` for canvases taller than wide, stacked layouts and squat landscapes (up to 500px tall and narrower than `SQUAT_LANDSCAPE`, 1.55:1), unless a shot has its own `squat` variant for those; on letterboxed desktops (`letterboxShare` above 0) a shot's own `letterbox` variant comes first; for other canvases up to 500px tall, `compact` under 600px wide (The watch) or `landscape`; none elsewhere. index.js applies the film's phone text band only in a stacked layout.
  The late terrain's root-shading probe uses the same bounded program helper through the internal rendering owner's `compilePrograms` member, keeping that helper out of the lazy chunk's imports. Terrain also checks the renderer's material-property store identity, so a context loss and restore between polls invalidates the old probe. A restored context prepares a fresh probe before the shading's commit point. Each detached material stays owned until its bounded poll settles and the ground's first draw takes its program lease; terrain teardown removes its draw hook and releases the probe. The shaded program is the longest link of a first visit (several seconds through ANGLE's D3D compiler on Windows), so a Lantern opening starts it early: once the ground's maps are final, film-scene.js `prelinkGround()` links it on a detached stand-in over the ground's own geometry (terrain-build.js `prelinkSlateGround()`), beside the terrain's build, and the probe then takes the same program from Three's cache. Both links, and the state check that accepts them, run with the ground's ancestors shown (terrain-build.js `withChainShown()`): The watch hides the environment and with it the lantern's light, and a program linked without that light would miss the cache at the cut's first draw and compile again there, blocking (3.4–5.2 s on every Watch opening until 2026-10-28). The probe's poll waits up to `LINK_WAIT_MS` (12 s) of visible time rather than the warm-ups' 2 s, so a slow compile is not left to the switch's draw. In a hidden opening the terrain commits while its program still links (nothing draws the ground before the opening's final warm-up, which waits for it). The ground's math-only loops (the river's runs and the crumbs' and laces' cells) run to a uniform bound (mud-ground.js `SLATE_LOOP`, always 1), so FXC keeps them as loops rather than unrolling every inlined copy: the same iterations, about a quarter off that link. The rocks link only their own programs (rock-build.js `compile(root)`), never the whole scene, which would catch the ground just before its shaded switch.

- **Commit points.** A change that would pop mid-shot lands only before the reveal, inside the fade-in, on a tour cut under the dissolve's kept frame, or on a still frame while the tour is not running: the lantern, the rocks, the film terrain, the slate's root shading and adaptive quality steps (`createDeferredQualityStep`, waiting at most 30 s). The mountains fade in instead. While the walk holds the tour, its own cuts in and out are the tour's (index.js `liveView`). A cut to one of the tree's shots waits for the film's own ground: until film-scene.js `landed` (or `failed`), the tour stays on the outgoing shot's last frame (camera-tour.js `update({ cutReady })`, `waiting`, `waited`), at most 20 s (index.js `GROUND_WAIT_S`). The ground may land during that wait (terrain-build.js `unseen()`); in practice the shot is The watch after a Watch opening, which shows no ground. Without it, a phone or a laptop cut from The watch to Portrait over the placeholder ground.
- **Coastal terrain.** film-scene.js keeps its borrowed ground while the lazy terrain owner prepares the coastline. On the live scene's `prepareOnly` path, `preparedEarthSteps()` extends the existing terrain slice scheduler with `coastGeometrySteps()`: cliff columns and stack rows prepare off-scene before the terrain promise resolves. Then `tuckUnderShelf()` settles every ground bed vertex the cliff's shelf covers to 0.06 beneath its top surface. The shelf samples the ground at its own coarse vertices, and between them the river's banks beside the falls otherwise rose through it in ragged pale patches. The water's cap has its own vertices and keeps its height. The detached terrain owns the resulting `{ cliff, stacks, tier }` record until `createCliff()` consumes those exact buffers and clears the record; cancellation or disposal frees anything not consumed. The cliff owner then owns their disposal. Synchronous builders retain their byte-identical output, and no map or shader begins during geometry preparation. Terrain geometry, the slate shading, sea, cliffs, stacks, falls and `SEA_SINK` land together at an existing unseen commit point. `filmScene.ready` publishes the prepared height sampler. The close-opening gate awaits its coastal commit, root and bank preparation and full foreground coverage through the current move. Hidden priority slices advance `rendering.openingProgress` only when a generator completes work; the gate uses that signal to distinguish a long active build from a stall. Camera measurement and fitting expose cooperative generators; synchronous cuts drain those same operations. A hidden opening completes its current fit through those short steps before applying the camera or starting subject and coverage readiness; pending work invalidates an older opening generation and requests another preparation frame. Visible cuts and resizes keep the synchronous fallback. Preparation snapshots the subject matrix and safe area, cancels stale work and caches only completed results; a missing lazy helper leaves the synchronous cut fallback available. Foreground generators yield before each seed and after at most four triangle footprint checks; water-cap clipping scans yield every 16 triangles. Hidden terrain and foreground preparation yield after 4 ms through the lazy preparation task owner, using cancellable scheduler tasks or a closed-on-completion MessageChannel rather than clamped nested timers. A hidden page suspends generator work; cancellation, stale generations and errors close the generator and queued resources. Revealed work returns to its existing idle scheduling. `filmScene.prepareOpening(frame)` forwards the final camera, viewport, current shot and actual next-shot context to that owner; `openingState` reports terrain, detail, foreground and failure state. Close openings start rock-scatter.js through `prepareOpening()` before reveal; other openings retain the reveal, film, settled-tree and high/balanced gates. Its lazy module and the terrain promise start independently. The builder starts both startup-tier GLBs without waiting for terrain preparation. Only placement waits for the resolved callable sampler and its own `ready` at the first coastal commit. Its internal `setActive()` hook withdraws the whole tuple when film deactivates and reuses its prepared resources at an eligible cut on reactivation. Its height delegates to procedural ground and its shore distance becomes infinite until landing and while withdrawn, so walking follows the visible surface. Rock placement takes its stable `film` sampler instead: later GLB completion cannot seat permanent rocks against the withdrawn procedural surface. The scatter owner immediately observes both the terrain promise and its eventual readiness, even if its lazy import fails or disposal wins. The builder owns completed model resources while waiting, aborts pending requests on disposal, releases late/completed sources once, and suppresses late failure status after disposal. Program preparation and the existing cut-only rock commit still follow placement. Disposal restores borrowed geometry, retires pending program polls, resolves uncommitted coastal readiness as false, and frees the derived shore resources.
- **Coast detail.** The lazy cliff owner borrows the ground's complete material for the shelf and owns its face/stack material. `coast-detail.js` creates independent optional owners for a shared 512px sRGB albedo (77,902 bytes, a 96 KiB streamed-body limit) and a shared 256px packed `NoColorSpace` normal/roughness tile (48 KiB limit). Each has an 8 s deadline and requests only after reveal; neither holds the cinematic reveal or waits for the other. The albedo is divided by its measured linear RGB mean and clamped to relative reflectance before mixing with the calibrated rock colour. Both textures reach the near-coast ground's uniforms (the material's `coastRockUniforms`) as the cliff's own bind and withdraw: exposed shelf bedrock takes the rock's colour and its detail normal in place of the soil's relief, in two turned lookups (`COAST_BEDROCK`), blending into slate soil inland; RG normal X/Y and B roughness remain data. Each owner stages its result until a tour cut or a still frame, leaves procedural shading usable on failure, and adds no pass or light. Withdrawal resets its own bindings and aborts pending work, while decoded textures and bitmaps remain cached for reactivation and context restoration. Adaptive steps reuse both fixed assets. Disposal withdraws the bindings before freeing each owned texture and bitmap; the borrowed ground material and its maps are never freed by the cliff, and the terrain restores the ground's previous uniform binding on teardown.
- **Holds.** Nothing on the page holds a revealed scene; only a visitor pause, reduced motion and the walk do. Before reveal, the independent preparation gate holds camera phase zero and tour time. `cameraTour.update({ ready: false })` excludes preparation time, and `reset()` grants the selected opener a full native dwell when it becomes visible. `scene.setVisitorPaused()` holds the tour (a tour hold) and, from the reveal, rendering (a render hold, the scheduler's `"visitor"` hold, `createVisitorHold`); `scene.isVisitorPaused()` reads it. Reduced motion holds the tour and the drift. The walk ([Scene modes](SCENE-MODES.md#walk)) holds the tour from its capture until its dissolve back ends (`cameraTour.update({ held })`), and a visitor pause or reduced motion ends a walk at once. The lazy walk owner also exits immediately when forced colours become active, restoring page visibility and releasing its mouse capture even before its first cut; disposal removes that media listener. Behind a visitor pause a resize, context restore or content change (model, map, shader, font) draws one still frame; scroll never does.
- **Failure.** If the watch's stage fails or the tree cannot load, `failToTitle()` stops the scene at once (a host never shown is hidden; a shown canvas fades back to the title card) and disposes the runtime on a later tick, once in-flight warm-ups settle. There is no fallback world. A lantern failure keeps the stand-in lantern: an iron post lantern that [architecture.js](../src/scene/architecture.js) builds in code beside the tree, not part of any model.
- **Diagnostics.** `?sceneDebug=1` publishes a read-only status object, `BabelSite.sceneDebug`; [Scene modes](SCENE-MODES.md) lists its fields. It also enables Three's shader error checks. Normal visits disable those diagnostic checks, avoiding r160's synchronous shader/program log queries at first use; shaders and rendering quality stay the same. There are no developer controls; the walk is the visitor's, unannounced, and never a debug camera.

- **Coastal fracture profile.** `cliffGeometrySteps()` retains the established modest lip first, then blends a stronger crown and upper-face profile (`lipProfile()`) over deterministic world-space slabs. Crown vertices move down and seaward along the fault's fixed normal, preserving their alongshore coordinate. A top-strip slope constraint prevents the extended surface from rising above its old walk-side plane. A fixed high-tier face reference keeps the crown, bevel and first face row ordered on both tiers. Inner shelf rows 0–5 retain their positions and UVs; the outer row's incident normals may respond to the moved crown. The deeper face, attached formations, indices, groups and counts remain unchanged. The complete waterfall slot, feather and 1.2-unit collar retain every attribute, including normals: the strong difference starts beyond an additional 1.2001-unit guard, wider than one balanced column, while its baseline neighbours keep the original modest geometry. Changed face vertices are projected seaward of signed coast distance 0.28. The existing terrain preparation, cut-time publication and disposal own the result. No new asset, program, light or pass is introduced.

## Rendering and quality

The scene runs on the high and balanced tiers only; the low tier keeps the title card. quality.js picks the startup tier: a data-saver request, 2 GB or less of device memory, or weak limits (textures under 4096 or anisotropy under 4) give low; four cores or fewer, a phone-sized touch viewport, a touch device without flagship limits, or a short side under 340 px give balanced; the rest get high. `?quality=` overrides it and pins the governor: an explicit tier takes no adaptive step.

|                     | High      | Balanced |
| ------------------- | --------- | -------- |
| Pixel-ratio cap     | 1.5       | 1.25     |
| Shadows             | 2048 map  | 1024 map |
| Bloom               | on        | off      |
| Scene MSAA (WebGL2) | 4 samples | none     |
| Stars               | 4200      | 2600     |

Touch-primary devices cap the pixel ratio at 1.25 on every tier. An integrated GPU on a mouse-and-keyboard machine takes the lean path (quality.js `isLean()`, from webgl-probe.js `gpuClass`, which classes the unmasked renderer string: Intel UHD, HD and Iris, AMD Radeon Graphics, Vega and 7xxM/8xxM, Adreno, Mali, Apple's base M chips and GeForce MX read as integrated): balanced's profile and assets at DPR 1 (`profile.lean`), its own cuts in the owners that read the flag (one mist step of one octave, one nebula layer, no maelstrom) and resolution steps of 0.85, 0.7 and 0.6. Every balanced startup (phones, the lean path) takes the lite ground and lens (index.js `liteGround`, fixed by the startup tier so a desktop's runtime step links nothing new): mud-ground.js `configureGroundShading({ lite })` gives the ground its own `+lite` program (`SLATE_LITE`: no close soil, the key's shadow filtered over one bilinear texel, no mirror in still pools, no lantern glints on the moving water, the stream foam's far share for its lace), the final pass takes no shot lens blur and no colour fringe (postprocess.js `uLens.y`), the mist marches one step of one octave (drifting-mist.js `setMistTier("lean")`), and the slate's grit and relief maps are never requested. Every profile may carry a pixel budget (`resolveEffectiveDprCap`, never below DPR 0.5): 2.1 MP on the lean path, 3.7 MP on high, so a 4K monitor never shades eight million multisampled pixels. The composer's targets follow the canvas in device pixels; the renderer's own antialiasing is off. Star sprites, bloom and the ink contour are sized in CSS pixels. Models and slate maps keep the startup tier (`assetTier`), so a quality step never re-downloads them. The optional coastal albedo and packed detail tiles are fixed at 512px and 256px respectively on both live tiers and never re-download on an adaptive step.

The governor samples display intervals only after the reveal, and not for 750 ms after a resize, resume, context restore, pause release or asset commit; a dissolve's tour capture, cut and first blended frames are skipped. A 30-frame window (after 15 warm-up samples) averaging over 20 ms for 45 consecutive frames steps down one tier. A revealed scene never draws the low profile: its steps below balanced, the resolution steps, keep the profile and lower only the pixel ratio (to 1, then 0.85; on the lean path 0.85, 0.7, then 0.6). Before the reveal the opening takes the same steps from its own hidden frames ([Opening presentation](#opening-presentation)). Recovery needs 300 frames whose window average stays under the larger of 15 ms and 1.1 times its 10th percentile, counted only while that percentile is at most 17.5 ms (a display at 60 Hz or faster that keeps up), and 10 s since the last change; each recovery followed by another downgrade doubles both, and after two such oscillations recovery stops. While the tour runs, a step waits for the next cut; where the browser links programs in parallel, it links them first (`rendering.prepareQuality()`). Each shot keeps its own level ([shot-levels.js](../src/scene/shot-levels.js) `enter()`, called by index.js on each cut and at the reveal, keyed by subject and angle, through quality.js `takeLevel()`): a step taken on a heavy shot lowers that shot's resolution for its later visits, a shot not yet seen starts at the level the tour holds, and a shot whose visit kept up (at most 5% of its sampled frames over 20 ms, over at least 60) climbs one step for its next visit, unless a climb has already failed there. Each cut starts the governor's window afresh, and once levels are per shot its own recovery stops: climbing is the shots'. Only balanced and its resolution steps are kept per shot, so they change only the pixel ratio; a desktop's step from high to balanced holds for every shot. A pinned `?quality=` keeps one level. `?perf=1` shows the frame rate, the longest gap, the shot, the pixel ratio and drawn megapixels and the governor's level in the window's corner ([perf-readout.js](../src/scene/perf-readout.js)), for checking a real phone or laptop.

Refresh estimation averages the last 32 callback intervals by their estimated number of display ticks; the 30th percentile identifies missed-vsync multiples rather than supplying the rate itself. It checks every 16 samples, snaps within 4% of a supported rate (including 175 Hz), and adopts after two agreeing estimates. A neighboring estimate within 10% of the adopted rate needs eight confirmations, so brief callback jitter cannot alternate the render divisor. Larger monitor changes retain the two-estimate response. Resetting drops samples and confirmations while keeping the adopted rate.

[runtime.js](../src/scene/runtime.js) renders only visible frames, on an even cadence: every nth vsync of the estimated refresh rate near 60 Hz (144 Hz draws 72 fps; touch screens stay at or below 60). The key's shadow map (`rendering.lights.sun`) redraws only after a shot (a shot's own sun turns the key), quality, resize, model or treatment change, or a restored WebGL context; a step between the tiers' map sizes frees the map for the next draw to allocate. The renderer uses `PCFSoftShadowMap`, whose directional-light filter in three r160 is a fixed 3×3 texel footprint that ignores `shadow.radius`, so the scene sets no radius: the map's texel size alone sets how soft a shadow's edge falls. The coast's own shaders read the same map: [ocean.js](../src/scene/ocean.js) `lighting()` lends its texture and matrix per draw (`seaShadow()`, a 16-tap bilinear PCF that fades in before the map's edge), the cliffs and stacks cast into it, and each of the fault's shots centres the film's shadow camera on its rock (`COAST_SHOTS` `shadow`). Before the watch commits, [night-environment.js](../src/scene/night-environment.js) prepares its environment while models download: it awaits the sky program, allocates the pinned r160 PMREM generator's blur material through its `_setSize` and `_allocateTargets` hooks, and awaits the blur and background programs before the unchanged `fromScene()` capture. Real renderer polls snapshot the program objects rather than r160's mutable material properties, stop on disposal or context loss, and time out after 2 s per link phase; a timeout leaves the established on-demand capture available. CompileAsync-only adapters retain their polled resources until the underlying operation settles. The watch's stage awaits `environment.ready`, so film activation borrows a finished capture. Its sky shader, cube size and filtering are unchanged; rendering.js draws it again on a cut that changes the sky's plate or the key (`refreshEnvironment()`) and recaptures after a lost context ([below](#sky-stars-and-far-plain)). The window's `resize` and a ResizeObserver on the host feed the resize controller (runtime.js `createSceneResizeController`), which resizes the renderer with `setSize(width, height, false)` when the size or the pixel ratio changed. A pixel-ratio change that resizes nothing, such as a window moved to a display of another scale, reaches it through runtime.js `createPixelRatioWatcher()`: index.js wires it to the controller, and it listens to a `(resolution: <ratio>dppx)` media query, re-arms on the new ratio after each change, then calls the controller. `disposeHomeSceneRuntime()` removes its listener. Without `matchMedia`, or a query without `addEventListener`, it watches nothing. rendering.js checks the WebGL context before entering the composer, so a lost context is caught before Three.js hears of it. The page normally opens two WebGL contexts, the probe and the renderer, both with `powerPreference: "default"`. When the probe could not read the texture limits, the scene opens a third, short-lived one for them (quality.js `readWebGLQualityCaps`). The capability probe normally runs in the worker; the renderer stays on the document thread. Each probe releases its context through `WEBGL_lose_context`.

### Materials and grade

The final pass's focus taps read level zero from the composer's and the kept frame's linear-filtered, non-mipmapped textures. WebGL2 provides explicit LOD; WebGL1 requests `EXT_shader_texture_lod`. This makes the sharp-subject shortcut independent of implicit texture derivatives inside varying control flow, avoiding an ANGLE/D3D11 compiler warning. Without that extension WebGL1 retains the original full sampling loop. The targets, filters, focus, layer tests and pass count are unchanged.

The live film keeps the sky's displayed palette and continuous cloud shade. Other layers dissolve continuously with the existing soft boundaries and subject/gap handling. Photographic grain, halation and lens effects remain in the existing passes. The shot's focus (`setLens()`) blurs in the final pass from the same codes: the sky and ranges by the shot's radius, the ground by a thin lens's circle of confusion from the plain's depth along each pixel's ray (its inverse affine in UV, `uGround`, with the kept frame's copy for the dissolve), nearer the lens than the focus at most the shot's `lens.near` radii (`uBlurNear`, `LENS_NEAR` by default), with no depth buffer or pass. The film's light is golden hour's ([film-light.js](../src/scene/film-light.js) `FILM_LIGHT`: a low warm key, the code's `moon`, a dusk-blue hemisphere over a brown ground bounce, a warm haze as the fog's colour). index.js sets the shot on screen's light on every frame, and it changes only on a cut: its mood (`shotLight()`) and look (`shotLook()`) through rendering.js `setShotLight()` and `applyShotTone()`, its warm `fill` (Close-up's, `setShotFill()`, which turns the fill light and tints it), and through `commitShotSky()` its `sun`: rendering.js `setShotSun()` turns the key to come from it and warms it (`SUN_KEY`), writes its direction to `SHOT_KEY.babelKeyDir`, which the ground reads to know the key from the warm lantern, and redraws the shadow map; the star moves to that direction from the camera, the mist's lights follow, the sky commits the shot's plate and the environment is drawn again. Each mountain GLB carries three material textures in standard slots: sRGB albedo in the base-color slot, an object-space normal map, and a `NoColorSpace` occlusion texture whose R channel is ambient occlusion and G is snow coverage. The main film river has a physical bed and a separate upper cap in the existing ground geometry/material draw. The near course in `RIVER` and `SLATE_RIVER` follows a smooth western arc that keeps the rendered main water at least 6 units from the lantern, leaving the former foreground reach as soil. The builder refines and constrains the wet bed, clips the cap against its actual bank intersections and uses wet-bed polygons through tight bends to avoid offset-strip folds. `riverSurfaceSteps()` extends only its water-cap polygon window 12 units west and south of the root window, covering the relocated bend and coarse bank edges with actual wet-bed polygons; solid refinement and the root lattice stay unchanged. Refinement, carving, cap construction and attribute installation yield through the terrain's existing cooperative scheduler, with runtime ceilings of 78,000 solid and 8,000 water triangles. The same terrain owner commits, withdraws and disposes the combined geometry.

The winding stream remains part of that terrain and ground-material owner. `STREAMS` in terrain-build.js mirrors `SLATE_STREAMS` in mud-ground.js: one approximately 23-unit course preserves the first five treeward controls and their water profile, then bends across the clearing to an inlet on the western main river. - **Sky shell.** [estate-sky.js](../src/scene/estate-sky.js) builds the shell's material, `createEstateSkyMaterial(config, textGuard)`. [atmosphere.js](../src/scene/atmosphere.js) drives its drift clock (`uTime`, on scene time) and its octaves (`uNebulaLayers`: 3 on high, 2 on balanced, 1 on the lean path, 0 outside the film), and [film-scene.js](../src/scene/film-scene.js) turns `uFilm` on. It writes no depth and draws last in the opaque list (`renderOrder` 1000) at the far plane (its vertex shader sets `gl_Position.z = gl_Position.w`), so the cloud field shades only the pixels the ground, tree, water and ranges leave open; the picture is the same as drawing it first. The film's cloud field is GLSL text, `cloudFieldGLSL(time)`, which the shell interpolates and the stars evaluate too. Its reshaping, `CLOUD_RESHAPE`, works through two weights, both exactly 0 over the reference banks. The open weight (`open`, on the bank density) also leaves the roof's clear lane alone. The bend weight (`bend`, on the noise's coordinates) eases in over azimuths at least as wide and a wider radius, and also reaches the lane, which keeps its own clearing. ## Build pipeline

[build.mjs](../build.mjs) writes `dist/` through [tools/build-output.mjs](../tools/build-output.mjs), which stages the payload, replaces assets before HTML and rolls back if publication fails.

- **Scripts.** The scene builds as ESM with code splitting: the entry, one shared chunk that may hold only third-party code (the build refuses a first-party module there, since it would evaluate before the entry's ordered imports), and lazy chunks. Chunks are named after the hash of their final bytes, dependencies first, so any change yields a new entry URL. The capability worker builds next as an independent IIFE. The UI then builds as one IIFE that names the worker URL and the entry's static chunks for preloading. The scene entry receives the model and slate manifests as string literals (`__BABEL_ARCHITECTURE_URLS__`, `__BABEL_MATERIAL_URLS__`); the UI receives only each tier's early tree and lantern URLs and sizes. The separate scalars `__BABEL_COAST_MATERIAL_URL__` and `__BABEL_COAST_ALBEDO_URL__` are read only in the lazy coastal chunk, keeping both optional URLs out of the entry and the slate manifest.
- **Fingerprints.** Scripts, CSS, every model, the slate maps, both coastal material maps and the fonts publish only as `name.HASH.ext` (eight hex digits of SHA-256), immutable for a year. The fonts are discovered from `fonts/*.woff2`. The stylesheet's font `url()`s are rewritten before minifying, from LF bytes, and the pages' asset paths (the stylesheet, the scripts and the font preloads) with exact replacements; an un-hashed stylesheet, script or font path, or a `?v=` query, left after the rewrite fails the build.
- **Legal comments.** esbuild keeps license comments at the end of each output (`legalComments: "eof"`); the shared chunk ends with Three.js's notice.
- **Shader compaction.** [tools/shader-compact.mjs](../tools/shader-compact.mjs) strips indentation, comments and spaces beside punctuation from the scene's GLSL template literals before esbuild minifies; Three.js's own shaders are untouched.
- **Static files.** The hosting, icon and discovery files in `public/` are copied verbatim (`public/site-agents.md` becomes `/AGENTS.md`); `sitemap.xml`'s `lastmod` comes from `public/index.md`'s `dateModified`. `fonts/OFL.txt` publishes beside the fonts.
- **Development.** `npm run dev` ([tools/dev.mjs](../tools/dev.mjs)) watches the inputs ([tools/watch.mjs](../tools/watch.mjs)), serializes rebuilds and serves `dist/` through an owned Wrangler process. Watch builds pass `--retain-assets`, which keeps, for pages already open, the hashed scripts, CSS, images, models and fonts that the folder's previous build recorded in its manifest (`.cache/build-outputs/`); without it a build removes the files its predecessor recorded and it no longer writes, and a file no build recorded is never removed. A failed build keeps the last good output. `--outdir` writes elsewhere than `dist/`; build-output.mjs `validateOutputDirectory` refuses the project root or any folder containing it, `src`, `images`, `fonts`, `public`, `tools`, `test`, `node_modules`, `.git`, `.cache` or any folder inside them, a path through a symbolic link, and an existing file, and `publishBuild` refuses a non-empty folder other than `dist/` that is not an earlier output of this project.

## Tests

`npm test` runs `node --test "test/*.test.mjs"`: Node's own runner, no framework. Files are grouped by domain: page and UI (`markup-accessibility`, `hero`, `contact`, `scene-loader`, `boot`, `webgl-probe`), scene wiring (`scene-bootstrap`, `scene-basics`, `scene-runtime`, `scene-quality`, `scene-postprocess`, `scene-visitor-pause`, `startup-cost`), camera (`cinematic`, `framing`, `coast-framing`, `camera-tour`, `opening-star`), subjects and surroundings (`architecture`, `architecture-preparation`, `architecture-assets`, `lantern`, `sky`, `night-environment`, `hill-silhouette`, `mountain-massifs`, `lightning`, `film-scene`, `ground`, `terrain-build`, `foreground-detail`, `soil-roughness`, `ocean`, `coast-detail`, `coast-geometry`, `coast-material`, `coast-waterfall`, `river-channel`, `rock-startup`, `coast-preparation`, `coast-handoff`), delivery (`build`, `bundle-output`, `hosting`, `smoke-pages`, `workflows`), and the docs (`docs`). `bundle-output` builds the site once into a temporary folder under `.tmp-preview-review/` and checks the real payload; two of its tests build small fixture projects there as well (the refused shared chunk, and a changed model's effect on the UI bundle).

[test/support/](../test/support/) holds shared helpers, outside the test glob. Each of `code.mjs`, `css.mjs` and `html.mjs` has its own whitespace-normalizing `flat()` for its language, so source, style and markup checks survive formatting; `code.mjs` also reads source files, and `css.mjs` parses rules and media blocks and computes contrast. `glb.mjs` is a GLB reader that handles normalized integer attributes, byte strides and node transforms, `terrain.mjs` holds the plain-dune baseline the terrain tests compare against, `ground.mjs` the live scene's analytic ground for the framing fixtures, and `contact-dom.mjs` is the fake footer the contact and boot tests run contact.js over.

### Opening presentation

The functional opening settles its sky plate or procedural fallback, then includes a final `presentation` stage after the final shaders. Its resource orchestration and lantern-light prediction use the existing lazy preparation chunk; the entry lends constructors to avoid a second scene-module chunk.
`opening-presentation.js`, delivered in the existing lazy preparation helper, observes only
real completed scene draws at the final opening pose: four of them (`OPENING_FRAMES`) and a
WebGL2 fence that signals without a blocking wait (WebGL1 retains linked-program checks),
whatever their pace: the scene opens as soon as it can draw (the owner's call of
2026-10-27). Their fastest interval says whether the profile fits, since an upload, a late
link or a skipped frame only lengthens a gap: over 40 ms (index.js `OPENING_FRAME_MS`) the
scene takes one governor step down while still hidden (quality.js `stepDown()`: a tier, or
below balanced the pixel ratio) and measures again; at the floor it opens as it draws.
Refit, context loss and visibility changes retire the window; disposal frees its owned
fence, never querying a lost context. The existing visible-time stall and total
preparation budgets still apply.

The canvas and loader complete only after that admission. The paper waits for the canvas's
480 ms fade, remaining closed in a hidden tab, then starts its existing opening. Its `closed`
status holds the camera and tour at phase zero until the sheets begin parting. A tap, key or
keyboard focus parts held doors at once, and the doors' own cap parts them after 10 s of
visible time (the held CSS backstop is 11 s); a scene still preparing then fades in over the
title card, and its own deadlines (the functional 15 s stall / 30 s total) still retire it.
The skip link, main and footer borrow `inert` while the doors are closed.

### Adaptive frame pacing

Auto quality evaluates a 30-render interval window after a 15-sample warm-up,
excluding 750 ms after uploads, resize, resume or restoration and three transition
samples. Forty-five sustained samples over 20 ms request relief, longer than the averaging window so one isolated hitch cannot trigger a step. High first requests
balanced per-frame settings; balanced then requests DPR 1 and 0.85 while retaining
its material profile. Models, terrain maps and sky images keep the startup asset tier.
The cap travels on the queued profile: a resize cannot publish it ahead of its cut.
A running tour or walk never accepts a step mid-shot, even after 30 s; hidden
preparation can accept it after shader preparation, retiring its opening draw window
and preparing the current pose again. Paused/still recovery retains a bounded escape.
Recovery remains display-floor-aware, at least 300 healthy samples and 10 s per rung,
with doubling back-off and two oscillations at most. Explicit quality overrides stay
fixed. The underlying governor's default options retain their previous behavior for
other callers; the live state opts into the faster window and two resolution rungs.

Initial environment preparation is singular: film activation cannot launch another
capture while its sky/filter programs are preparing. Ready captures bind only while
still current and owned. A changed hidden sky records a dirty reflection and refreshes
it only after outstanding scene shader warm-ups settle, before the final opening
warm-up. Freed programs are rejected before either readiness or link-status queries.

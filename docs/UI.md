# UI

The page's interface as built: its tokens, breakpoints, layers, components, motion, Contact and the 404 page. [Style](STYLE.md) sets the rules it follows, [Accessibility](ACCESSIBILITY.md) says how it meets them, [Content](CONTENT.md) holds the words, and [Contracts](CONTRACTS.md#dom) lists the DOM hooks the scripts and the scene share. The sources are [index.html](../index.html), [404.html](../404.html), [styles.css](../styles.css), [src/ui/](../src/ui/) and [main.js](../src/main.js). Terms such as title card, live path, reveal and render hold are defined in the [Glossary](GLOSSARY.md).

## Page structure

The homepage's `body`, in order:

1. The skip link (`.skip-link`, to `#main`).
2. The scene shell (`.scene-shell`, `aria-hidden`): the loading line's star (`#scene-star`, `hidden`), the scene host `#home-scene.scene-canvas`, then `.scene-vignette`. The star comes before the host, so the vignette can follow the host directly, as it must, because the title card's grain fades through a sibling selector.
3. The site shell (`.site-shell`, `aria-hidden`).
4. The opening's doors (`#scene-doors.scene-doors`, `aria-hidden`): two halves, `.scene-doors__leaf--left` and `--right`, each holding its paper (`.scene-doors__paper`, with the light `.scene-doors__light` and its `.scene-doors__glow`) and its torn lip (`.scene-doors__rim`). On the live path the doors' script adds their drawing, `canvas.scene-doors__canvas`, after the halves.
5. `main#main` (`tabindex="-1"`): the hero (`#home.hero.section`, holding `#hero-minimal`).
6. The bottom bar (`.bottom-bar`, `aria-hidden`) and its empty spacer.
7. The loading line (`#scene-loader`, `aria-hidden`, `hidden`): its label, then its bar (`.scene-loader__track` holding `.scene-loader__fill`).
8. The footer: the Contact button (`.site-footer__contact`, `hidden`) and its links (`#contact-links`: the email address and LinkedIn).
9. The UI bundle, `<script defer src="/scripts/app.js">`.

Neither page has an inline style: the Content Security Policy forbids them, and scripts change styles only through the CSSOM. Neither has an `<img>` or `<picture>`, and the stylesheet requests no picture: the night, its stars, the grain and the cursor are CSS gradients and data URIs, and its one image is the opening's paper detail, a material, which loads after the scripts and fonts the page preloads.

### Scene framing

The scene frames its subject around the hero and the bottom bar, so its layout rules mirror the hero's breakpoints. styles.css puts the name at the top on portrait screens (squares included) up to 1024px wide, and on landscape screens up to 760px wide or 500px tall; elsewhere it sits bottom-left.

**The stacked-layout helper.** `isStackedLayout(width, height)` in [directed-shots.js](../src/scene/directed-shots.js) says whether the name stacks above the subject. [cinematic.js](../src/scene/cinematic.js) re-exports it, and index.js reads it too.

```js
height >= width ? width <= 1024 : width < 600 && height > 500;
```

That is every portrait or square screen up to 1024px wide, and landscape screens narrower than 600px and taller than 500px. On the other landscape screens whose hero sits at the top (600 to 760px wide, or up to 500px tall: phones turned sideways), the scene frames the subject beside the name, as on desktop. Change the helper with the hero's breakpoints; `test/cinematic.test.mjs` holds sizes on each side of it.

| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `cinematicSafeArea(width, height, hero, nav)`                    | cinematic.js                                                                | The box the shot fits into. ## Components

### Title card

The first paint on every path and the last on the static path ([Glossary](GLOSSARY.md)). It needs no script and requests no image.

- **Night.** `body`'s vertical gradient, `--night-900` to `--night-800` at 34% to `#0d1119`, under `.scene-shell`'s own background: two faint cool hazes, one where the moon sits in the opening shot (78% across, 16% down), over a veil that darkens toward the foot.
- **Star field.** `.scene-shell::before`: two tiling SVG data URIs of 40 stars each, on 640px and 1040px tiles that never line up. The stars are warm (`#efe8da`) with a few cool ones (`#d6dee8`), 0.4 to 1.1px in radius, and fade from 36% of the height to nothing at 84%. They and the hazes lie under the canvas, so only the title card shows them.
- **Vignette.** `.scene-vignette`: two faint pale glows in the upper left and upper centre, an elliptical darkening toward the edges and a vertical darkening toward the foot. It stays over the live scene.
- **Grain.** `.scene-vignette::before`: a 150px tile of fractal noise (`feTurbulence`, seed 7) split into faint white and black specks whose alphas balance near code value 23, on the night, so 8-bit screens show the gradients dithered rather than stepped. It lies above the canvas and below all text. At the reveal `#home-scene` gains `is-ready`, and `.scene-canvas.is-ready + .scene-vignette::before` fades the grain out with the canvas's 480ms fade-in; no `:has()` is needed. When the scene drops `is-ready` (a lost WebGL context, a failure after the reveal or teardown), the canvas fades out and the grain returns. It stays on the static path and on the 404, and is removed in forced colours and increased contrast.
- **Site shell.** `.site-shell`: faint vertical rules every 72px and a pale wash at the top, masked to fade out by 72% of the height, at opacity 0.34.

### The opening's doors

`#scene-doors` ([scene-doors.js](../src/ui/scene-doors.js); [Architecture](ARCHITECTURE.md#the-openings-doors) has its lifecycle): one sheet of weathered parchment torn down the middle, a pirate's map, the owner's picks of 2026-10-25, closed over the whole page at the first paint, the identity, Contact and loading line under it, and parted onto the scene and the words together ([Style](STYLE.md#homepage-title-card-and-footer)).

- **Halves.** Each `.scene-doors__leaf` is a whole-window box (`inset: 0`). Its `.scene-doors__paper` is clipped to its side of the tear by a `polygon()` of 81 points, `calc(50% ± Npx)` across and `%` down, so the tear keeps its px jag on any width; the two overlap by 0.6px so no hairline shows. Its `.scene-doors__rim` is the tear's lip, a band inside its own edge: the left's pale fibres (`rgba(214, 196, 164, 0.5)`, warming toward `rgba(255, 190, 116, 0.42)` with the square of the load's light, `--doors-light`, which the script writes), the right's in its shade (`rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.32)`).
- **Paper.** Parchment `#a47744` under a lamp's pool at the top (`rgba(255, 232, 186, 0.62)`), the creases of quarter folds at 25%, 75% and half height, tide-lined stains from a seamless noise (`data:` URI, 1600px), a darkening toward the edges and an inset shadow that chars the window's edges (never the tear, since each half's box is the whole window), matched to the drawn parchment; `::after` lays the scan's detail tile (`/images/paper/paper-detail.webp`, 768px, one sheet's fibres across the tear) over it and its light with `mix-blend-mode: overlay`, so the fibres show where light comes through.
- **Light.** `.scene-doors__light` fills its half; its `.scene-doors__glow` is a 100px box at the star's centre, the camera sun's warm white at its heart (`rgba(255, 236, 215, 0.9)`), warmed through the parchment as it spreads and gone by its edge, which the script translates and scales to seven star radii (at least 64px) as the star warms, with the loading line's star.
- **Bar.** The loading line lies under the paper, so its bar shows on it, in ink: `::before` the faint track (`rgba(42, 25, 12, 0.18)`) and `::after` the fill (`#2a190c`), 2px along the top edge where the line's own bar is (below the status bar where the page runs under it), the fill scaled to the progress the script writes (`--doors-light`). They come in with the line's first word (`is-loading`, 320ms after 240ms) and fade over 240ms as the doors part, over the line's own bar.
- **Input.** While held, a tap on the paper, a key (not a modifier alone or a shortcut) or keyboard focus parts the doors at once, so no control the visitor reaches for waits under the paper.
- **Parting.** The left half turns −2.5° about its bottom-left corner and the right +2.5° about its bottom-right as each travels 58% of the width outward; each light turns and travels back about the same corner, so the glow stays put while the paper passes it.
- **Drawn.** On the live path the doors' script adds `canvas.scene-doors__canvas` (`inset: 0`, 100% of the window); it fades in over 520ms once drawn (`is-drawn`), and the halves then hide (`is-drawn-only`, `visibility: hidden`). The drawing parts on the same timing ([Motion](#motion)).
- **States.** `is-held` (the script holds them), `is-paused` (the page is hidden), `is-open` (parting), `is-drawn` and `is-drawn-only`; `hidden` once they have left.

### Loading line

`#scene-loader`, on the live path only ([scene-loader.js](../src/ui/scene-loader.js); [Architecture](ARCHITECTURE.md#title-card-and-loading-line) has its progress model): a label above the footer and a bar along the window's top edge, both in the name's colour, `--text` ([Style](STYLE.md#homepage-title-card-and-footer)). It takes no pointer events and cannot be selected.

- **Label.** `.scene-loader`, the label's box, is fixed and centred one 44px row above the footer's offset, `min(240px, 100% - 48px)` wide. It holds "Loading · N%" (`.scene-loader__label` with the value in `.scene-loader__value`) in the footer's meta type (12px/16px, 0.08em tracking, the meta shadow), with tabular figures and a fixed 4ch slot for the value.
- **Bar.** `.scene-loader__track`, fixed across the window's top edge (`right` and `left` 0, `top` the safe-area inset: 0 in a browser tab, below the status bar in the home-screen app, which runs under it), 2px tall, `rgba(247, 241, 234, 0.08)`. Its `.scene-loader__fill` covers it (`inset: 0`) in `--text` with a soft glow (`0 0 6px rgba(247, 241, 234, 0.3)`), scaled from its left edge (`transform-origin: 0 50%`), at `scaleX(0)` until the script writes it. The bar sits inside `#scene-loader`, so it fades in and out with the label.
- **Progress.** Only the value and the fill change, with real progress: at most once a frame, scene-loader.js writes the value's text and the fill's `transform`, `scaleX(p)` for the label's progress p, 1 at the reveal. The fill eases to each new value over 520ms (`cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)`), and over 240ms once the line is `is-done`.
- The same opening selector serves UI boot and the scene, including script selection. Actual preparation stages reserve 20% of the pre-reveal cap once reported; no timer advances the bar. The independent main/scene opening gate keeps the title card until a close opening is prepared and falls back after 15 visible seconds without progress or one 30-second total covering entry/shared downloads, setup and preparation, even if this decorative line is absent. Background and blocking preference pauses are excluded; timeout cancels unused early model requests and prevents late initialization.
- `begin()` unhides it and adds `is-loading` (fade in). `end()` adds `is-done` (fade out within the canvas's fade-in) and hides it 600ms later, clearing the fill's transform. The reveal completes it at 100%; a hidden scene host or 15 s without progress retires it below 100%.
- It has no pseudo-element layer, keyframes or animation of its own.

### Loading line's star

`#scene-star`, in the scene shell before the host: the scene's star as the opening shot will show it ([Style](STYLE.md#homepage-title-card-and-footer)). It stays `hidden` until the scene reports where The watch will show its star ([Architecture](ARCHITECTURE.md#title-card-and-loading-line)), and then warms with the loading line's progress.

- **Box.** `.scene-star` is absolutely placed at the shell's top-left with no size, at `z-index: 0`: above the shell's background and its star field, and under the canvas (1). It takes no pointer events. scene-loader.js translates it to the star's centre.
- **Parts.** Two spans, in order: `.scene-star__glow` and `.scene-star__disc`. Each is a 100px box centred on the star's centre (`top` and `left` −50px), at opacity 0 until the script writes it, and sized by its transform.
- **Disc.** The scene's star at the reveal (solar-body.js `SOLAR_LOOK`) as the canvas shows it beneath the same vignette: clipped white-hot, a little warm, `rgb(252, 251, 244)`, flat to its limb.
- **Glow.** Reaches two and a half radii (its box, the limb at 40%): the clipped light runs on past the limb at the disc's own colour, then falls away through gold (`rgba(255, 223, 185, 0.84)` at 1.25 radii, `rgba(255, 184, 132, 0.35)` at 1.5) and is gone by about 2.2. The lens's ghosts are the canvas's alone; they come in with its fade.

The star warms from the progress at which its place first became known (`since`) to the reveal: its warmth w is (progress − since) / (1 − since), and 1 at the reveal. For a star centred at (x, y) with radius r in a canvas `width` × `height`, all in CSS px, scene-loader.js writes:

| Part | `transform`                       | `opacity`                    |
| ---- | --------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Star | `translate(x, y)`                 |                              |
| Disc | `scale(2r / 100 × (0.4 + 0.6√w))` | √w: an unseen ember at first |
| Glow | `scale(5r / 100 × (0.5 + 0.5w))`  | w                            |

- **Reveal.** At the reveal w is 1: the star is the scene's own as the opening shot shows it, in place and size, and the canvas's 480ms fade-in covers it, a crossfade with nothing to time. It hides with the line 600ms after the run ends; a line retired below 100% hides it at once.
- **Writes.** Only the parts' `transform` and `opacity`, at most once a frame, with the value and the bar's fill. The star has no transition, animation, keyframes or pseudo-element of its own.
- **Preferences.** Forced colours and increased contrast hide it, as they hide the title card's grain. Reduced motion needs nothing for it: it changes only as the script writes it.

### Reduced motion

Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`:

- `transition: none` on the skip link, the hero block, the footer's controls (`.site-footer__link`: Contact and its links), the 404's Home link, the canvas, the title card's grain, the tablet intro's scrim and the loading line with its bar's fill. The canvas, the grain and the intro's scrim change at once at the reveal; the loading line appears and goes at once, and its bar steps to each new value. The loading line's star has no transition to remove, and the canvas, appearing at once, covers it at the reveal.
- `animation: none` on the hero block and the footer.
- No doors: `.scene-doors` is `display: none`, and the script leaves it alone.
- `scroll-behavior: auto`.
- hero.js holds the hero at full opacity with no transform, and follows the preference as it changes.
- main.js loads no live scene unless the address forces it ([Accessibility](ACCESSIBILITY.md#preferences)); a forced scene holds the tour and the drift.

## Contact

[contact.js](../src/ui/contact.js) defines `BabelSite.ui.initContact()`, which main.js's `initUi()` calls after `initHeroChrome()`. It turns the footer's links into Contact's disclosure ([Footer and Contact](#footer-and-contact)).

- **Binding.** It needs Contact (`.site-footer__contact`) and `#contact-links`, and binds only while focus is outside the links; otherwise it returns `false` and changes nothing, so the links stay as the no-JS fallback. Binding collapses the links (`hidden`, Contact's `aria-expanded="false"`), unhides Contact and returns `true`; a second call returns `true` and binds nothing more.
- **Toggle.** A click on Contact opens or closes the links, and `aria-expanded` follows.
- **Escape.** Escape closes open links. When focus was inside them it returns to Contact, without scrolling.
- **Hashes.** An address ending `#about`, `#profile`, `#experience` or `#contact`, or the same with `-text` (`#about-text` and so on), opens the links, on load and on every `hashchange`, so a link naming any of them lands on Contact. The hash must match exactly; any other, such as the skip link's `#main`, changes nothing, and none closes the links.
- **Nothing more.** It never writes the address, traps focus, makes anything `inert`, dims the page or holds the scene.

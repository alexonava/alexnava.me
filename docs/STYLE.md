# Style

The rules for visual, motion and typographic changes. Read them before changing how the site looks or moves.

## Principles

- Calm by design: quiet on load, clear on click. Atmospheric, never generic dark UI or visual noise.
- A motion picture with a painted sky (the owner's direction of 2026-10-14, "lean in slightly further to go for a motion picture film look with a paint brush sky theme"): every shot reads as a frame of film (`FILM_LOOK` in [postprocess.js](../src/scene/postprocess.js)): a red-orange halation bleeding about the brightest warm lights (the star, the lantern's flame; cool moon glints and moonlit bark keep their colour), a print film's toe lifting the blacks toward a cool, milky tint, fine grain strongest in the midtones, and the frame weaving a fraction of a pixel in its gate. The sky is painted with a brush (`CLOUD_BRUSH` in [estate-sky.js](../src/scene/estate-sky.js), `SKY_INK` in postprocess.js): the banks end in dry-brush strokes and carry faint bristle streaks, both laid along the clouds' own flow, their ink line thickening and thinning; the finest strokes never make a speck of their own, so no lone puff floats in clear sky and no pinhole opens in a bank, while the lanes, windows and pockets between the banks stay (`CLOUD_ISLANDS`). All of it stays slight.
- One gallery: every shot belongs to the same night, with photographic materials and light beneath one painted sky. Effects frame the scene quietly; they never flatten its natural surface detail into a painting.
- Depth comes from gradients, light and procedural texture, not UI chrome.
- Keep the current alignment and composition unless a task calls for a layout change.
- The sky and its clouds are the only artistic part of the environment: painterly, cel-stepped banks. Everything else aims for photorealism: subjects, mountains, ground, cliffs and water shade continuously, with no cel step, ink outline or added diffuse rim. Physical surfaces keep their local colour, roughness and normal variation. Only motivated light lights them: the moon, the night sky, the candle lantern and what bounces from them (the owner's 2026-10-19 "the sky is the only artistic element. everything else is photoreal"; where it meets an earlier stylised pick, realism wins). Their scene light passes one filmic tone curve and the display's encode (`FILM_TONE` in [film-light.js](../src/scene/film-light.js); the owner's pick of 2026-10-19, Deep): night photography's deep shade under bright moonlit faces, highlights rolling off toward white, never clipping, in a camera's night balance a little warm of the moon, colour dimmed as night's is; the painted sky and the star keep their colours as shown. Film grain and halation remain photographic finishing.

## Palette and type

- Deep navy, soot and twilight stone backgrounds; warm off-white text, never pure white; amber, brass and soft peach accents with an occasional cool twilight lift. No flat black, neon or saturated rainbow colour.
- Display type is Cormorant Garamond, body and UI type Instrument Sans: the two self-hosted variable fonts and no others.
- The name is Cormorant 700, `#f7f1ea`, with its soft halo, from the font's true 700 master with synthetic bold disabled; the 404's heading takes the same face, weight and halo. Eyebrows are uppercase, tracked and restrained.
- All text takes the name's colour, `#f7f1ea` (`--text`), a warm off-white (the owner's rule of 2026-10-15: "i kind of want all the text to be the color of my name because the color they are looks cheap"): the intro, the eyebrow, the footer, the loading line and the 404 included. Size, weight, tracking and the footer's underlines make the hierarchy, never a dimmer grey, a fade or a brighter state; only forced colours give text the system's colours. Gold belongs to the sun and materials, not secondary text. Controls use the shared surface, line and focus tokens; no bright blue chrome or glossy buttons.
- Selected text takes a translucent wash of the sun's amber, never system blue: warm white (`#f7f1ea`) over a 32% wash, one rule for both pages, reading above 4.5:1 on the night. Both pages declare `color-scheme: dark`.
- The name, the intro and the 404's heading balance their lines, and the 404's text wraps pretty, so none ends on a lone word.

## Motion and accessibility

- Motion is structural. Hover and focus may lift slightly but never change the text's colour: hover and a press answer with an underline. Nothing spins or jitters (the scene's film weaves a fraction of a pixel in its gate, the owner's pick of 2026-10-14; the page's text never moves). Hover styles apply only on devices that can hover (`(hover: hover)`), so a tap leaves no underline or lift behind; focus styles show everywhere, and a press underlines in full without lifting.
- Reduced motion removes movement: the page's entrances and transitions go, the opening's doors with them, and the live scene is not loaded.
- Text keeps 5:1 contrast or better on the night and over every tour shot. No text below 12px. Every interactive target is at least 44px: the skip link, the footer's Contact and its email and LinkedIn links, and the 404's Home.
- Safe-area insets keep the hero, the footer, the bottom bar and the loading line (its label, and its bar below the home-screen app's status bar) clear of notches and toolbars; the skip link clears the notch and hides wholly above it. `main` is at least one viewport tall and clips decorative overflow on both axes without becoming a scroll container, so the hero's scrim cannot create a scrollbar; in-flow content can grow the box and scroll with the document. Forced colours take system colours and the system cursor, show no doors, and block scene startup even with quality or debug overrides; increased contrast takes the system cursor and drops the title card's grain, the loading line's star and the doors' fibres, light and drawing, and changes nothing else. Reduced transparency removes the bottom scrim and keeps the text scrims (the hero's, the tablet intro's and the footer's); the loading line's track turns solid and its fill loses its glow.

## Materials and lighting

- Keep the supplied silhouettes, UVs and map resolutions. In film only the supplied maps' baked daylight highlights come down (15%, `FILM_GRADES` in [architecture.js](../src/scene/architecture.js)). The bare tree keeps its authored colour between shots, its normal map at 0.75 strength and its roughness map varied within 0.82–1.00: matte bark with local grain and fissures, not a smooth blue coat. Wood, bark and stone emit no light; only the lantern's luminous parts keep emission. Wetness stays at its base: below about 1.5 units above the soil roughness eases to 0.55, and where the roots and trunk enter the soil the bark is muddy, darker (about 0.53) and wetter (0.35). Bark glints behind the name, intro and Contact pass the existing text knees by each shot's guards. Add no leaves to the tree; fallen leaves on the ground are allowed. Damp, dark grey-green moss (`ROOT_MOSS` in [architecture.js](../src/scene/architecture.js)) grows in patches on the roots' sky-facing tops just above the mud line, never high on the trunk or saturated.
- Only motivated light: the moon key, the night sky (the environment) and the light it leaves in the shade (the fill, hemisphere and ambient), and the candle in the lantern, the one warm light; real cast shadows (the moon key's, on high and balanced) and dark recesses. Add no lights and no flattening ambient; the crown's old fill is gone.
- The film's moonlight is a real night's: the moon many times the sky's light (`FILM_LIGHT` in [film-light.js](../src/scene/film-light.js): key 2.4 over a fill of 0.3, hemisphere 0.4 and ambient 0.15), a cool white in the camera's balance, so its shadows fall deep, with real cast shadows; the tone curve's shoulder rolls highlights toward white rather than clipping them.
